# Chrome 3D icon art direction

Treat the approved references as the source of truth. Match their shared visual behaviour without copying one icon literally.

## Character and geometry

- Create premium, mature, approachable product illustration for a stock-broking experience.
- Keep the subject tactile but simplified, with a compact and immediately readable silhouette.
- Use substantial forms, moderate corner radii, and restrained bevels.
- Avoid gamified, inflated, candy-like, toy-like, or children’s-app styling.
- Avoid fragile detail, dense texture, and mechanically complex assemblies.

## Camera and composition

- Use a soft front three-quarter product view with slight elevation and mild long-lens perspective.
- Keep the meaning-bearing face or component clearly visible.
- Change facing direction only when it communicates the subject better.
- Avoid top-down isometric views, wide-angle distortion, and extreme foreshortening.
- Optically centre one object or one tightly related group at roughly 60–75% of a square canvas.
- Preserve generous transparent padding and one subtle neutral-grey grounding shadow.

## Materials

Choose materials according to the subject:

- warm-white woven fabric for bags and soft containers;
- thin, lightly fibrous paper for notes and documents;
- warm-white semi-matte ceramic or coated polymer for product surfaces;
- signature royal/electric blue in satin or controlled semi-gloss finishes;
- crisp satin or polished silver for rings, rods, rims, edges, and hardware;
- restrained glass or translucent faces for charts and screens;
- warm reflective gold only for bullion or commodity meaning;
- smooth semantic surfaces for market candles and state markers.

Use physically plausible material separation, broad controlled highlights, clean anti-aliasing, restrained textures, and controlled reflections. Do not cover the object in glossy plastic.

### Thin paper and currency

- Render every sheet as thin and flexible with minimal visible edge thickness.
- Build bundle thickness from a plausible stack of individually thin sheets with subtle layer separation.
- Allow a slight natural fan, bend, curl, or edge lift when useful.
- Never render notes or documents as padded cards, foam slabs, books, rigid blocks, or thick tiles.

### Crisp metal

- Give metal a clear reflective response, crisp edge highlights, and believable hardness.
- Keep reflections controlled enough to preserve the silhouette at icon scale.
- Never render metal as pillowy, rubbery, chalky, or soft plastic.

## Colour modes

### Standard mode

- Use warm white, silver, grey, and signature royal/electric blue as the core palette.
- Let a neutral material or blue carry the primary mass according to the subject.
- Use green and red only for market direction or state.
- Use orange only for date or time emphasis.
- Use gold only for bullion or commodity meaning.
- Use lime or yellow-green only for a necessary growth or forward-action metaphor.
- Never add accent colours as decoration.

### Empty-state mode

- Use warm white, silver, and neutral grey only.
- Do not use blue, green, red, orange, gold, lime, or any other accent.
- Communicate meaning through silhouette, pose, depth, material contrast, and value hierarchy.
- Keep the result calm, low-noise, and suitable for a no-data, waiting, unavailable, or zero-result state.

## Lighting and rendering

- Use bright diffused studio illumination with broad upper-left/front highlights.
- Add gentle fill, clean midtones, subtle ambient occlusion, and controlled specular edges.
- Prefer a premium physically based render over hyper-real photography.
- Preserve one soft neutral-grey semi-transparent contact shadow as partial alpha.
- Keep all remaining canvas pixels transparent.

## Avoid

- baked cards, scenery, gradients, or opaque backgrounds;
- generic inflated clay, candy, toy, or mascot styling;
- heavy dark metal or excessive mechanical detail;
- hard shadows, dramatic cinematic light, or noisy texture;
- decorative charts, labels, logos, or multiple unrelated objects;
- colour outside the selected mode.
